# The Instrument Argument

Every measurement system eventually measures itself. This essay argues that
the instruments a field builds to observe its subject end up shaping the
subject, and that the correction is editorial rather than technical.

## Objections

The first objection is definitional: an instrument that shapes its subject is
no longer an instrument. We take the point and keep the word, because the
alternative vocabulary is worse.

The second objection is empirical. The record does not show the drift the
argument predicts, or so the objection goes. The record does not show it
because the record is produced by the instruments in question.

## The Shape of the Evidence

The evidence arrives pre-formatted. A survey answers the questions it asked;
a benchmark rewards the behaviour it measures; a citation graph ranks what it
can see. The instruments a field builds to observe its subject end up shaping
the subject, and no amount of statistical care inside the pipeline corrects
for the pipeline.

What would count as disconfirming evidence here is a genuinely instrument-free
observation, and the essay's uncomfortable admission is that it cannot name
one.

## Objections

A third objection deserves its own section because it is the strongest: the
argument proves too much. If instruments always shape their subjects, the
claim applies to this essay's own method, and the essay saws off the branch
it sits on.

The reply is that the argument is a matter of degree. Some instruments shape
their subjects more than others, and knowing which is which is exactly the
editorial judgement the conclusion calls for.

## Conclusion

The correction is editorial rather than technical. A field that knows its
instruments are load-bearing reads its own record the way a good editor reads
a draft: asking not only what it says, but what the process that produced it
was capable of saying.
