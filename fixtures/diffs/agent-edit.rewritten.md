# On Reading Aloud

## Why the ear catches what the eye misses

Reading a draft out loud is the oldest revision technique in existence, and it
is still the most dependable. The reason is mechanical rather than mystical: the
eye will happily skim a sentence it has already seen four times, whereas the
mouth will not. A clause that doesn't work trips the tongue before it registers
as a problem on the page.

## The practice

Read the entire draft aloud at reading pace. Don't stop to fix things — note
where you stumbled and continue. Stopping turns the exercise into line-editing,
a different pass with a different goal. Reach the end: you're testing the shape
of the argument at speed, which is impossible if you never get to the final
page.

## What it won't catch

Reading aloud surfaces rhythm problems, buried subjects, and sentences that run
past the breath. It is nearly useless for structure. A document whose third
section belongs after its fifth will read perfectly well aloud, sentence by
sentence, right through to a conclusion that doesn't follow.
