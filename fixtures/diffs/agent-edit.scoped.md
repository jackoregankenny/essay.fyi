# On Reading Aloud

## Why the ear catches what the eye does not

Reading a draft aloud is the oldest revision technique there is, and it remains
the most reliable. The reason is mechanical rather than mystical: the eye is
willing to skim a sentence it has already read four times, and the mouth is not.
A clause that does not work will trip the tongue before it registers as a
problem on the page.

## The practice

Read the whole thing, out loud, at reading pace. Do not stop to fix things —
mark where you stumbled and keep going. Stopping turns the exercise into
line-editing, which is a different pass with a different purpose. Get to the
end: you are testing the shape of the argument at speed, and you cannot test
that if you never reach the last page.

## What it will not catch

Reading aloud finds rhythm problems, buried subjects and sentences that run past
the breath. It is close to useless for structure. A document whose third section
belongs after its fifth will read perfectly well aloud, one sentence at a time,
all the way to a conclusion that does not follow.
